# Attunetion App Review response

Apple rejected build 9 on October 5 under guidelines 4.3 and 4.2.6. If disputing the finding, Apple asked for a reply to the existing review message before resubmitting. Nathan’s approved reply was posted in the original App Review thread. App Store Connect showed two messages, with the new message attributed to Nathan Fennel.

[Original App Review correspondence](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/apps/6757886472/distribution/reviewsubmissions/details/c5f49241-6a36-4c95-9260-1c410e39a7bc)

## Reply sent to Apple

Thank you for reviewing Attunetion. Here are my answers about why I built it, how it was tested, and its relationship to my other apps.

1. **What problem does the app solve?** I built Attunetion because I would leave a meeting at work or a session with a trainer with a clear idea of what I wanted to focus on for the next day, week, or month. I wanted an easy way to keep track of those intentions and see them in my daily life. In Attunetion I can write the intention in my own words, choose how long it applies, and see my current focus in a Home Screen widget.
2. **Who uses it?** A friend introduced me to setting intentions. I found it helpful, and conversations with other friends showed me that they also wanted a way to keep their intentions in view. I built Attunetion for people like us, including someone learning to put an intention into words and someone who already knows what they want to focus on.
3. **What meaningful gap does it fill?** When my friends looked for an app, most of the options they found cost money. The free ones they tried felt much like taking a note. They also found the guided processes too rigid for someone learning to write an effective intention, and few of the apps they found had a widget. Those experiences shaped the design. In Attunetion you can write directly or start with the guide and edit the wording. You can choose a day, week or month and see your current intention on the Home Screen. That last part mattered to me because I wanted to see the intention during an ordinary day without having to remember to open the app.
4. **What human testing changed it?** Before submission, my friend who introduced me to intentions and I had both used Attunetion for months. Over several rounds of testing and feedback, I reworked how an intention gets from the app onto the Home Screen widget. Using the app also showed us a need for intentions that last longer than a day, which led me to add longer-term intentions. Feedback from testing also shaped the app’s design and themes.
5. **Is it standalone or part of a suite?** Attunetion is a standalone app. I don’t consider any of my other apps to be in the same category. The closest comparison I can think of is Giant Text, which serves a different purpose. Attunetion is where someone writes a day, week or month intention and keeps it in view over time.
6. **Could another title incorporate this functionality?** Someone opens Giant Text to type a one-time message and show it to another person in wiggly text as large as the display allows. Its widget is a shortcut back to the app. I built Attunetion separately because the person writing the intention is also the person who wants to see it again. They choose a focus for a day, week or month, style it to their taste, and can read it directly on their Home Screen throughout that period. Those ongoing personal intentions are the reason to open Attunetion and the reason its widget exists.
7. **What first-party code or assets are shared?** I did not reuse code, designs or artwork from Giant Text or any of my other apps when building Attunetion. Within Attunetion itself, its Apple platform versions share the intention model and parts of the SwiftUI interface.
8. **Which third-party templates or libraries were used?** I built Attunetion from scratch, without a third-party app template, starter project or app builder. The native Xcode project has no resolved third-party Swift package dependencies. The backend uses the OpenAI SDK for optional suggestions. The app icon, themes and other artwork are original to Attunetion. None came from another app or product.
9. **Who developed and owns the concept and branding?** I developed Attunetion as my own independent project. I publish my own projects through this developer account. I have never used it to publish on behalf of a client or another organization. I own the app icon and all branding created for Attunetion. I chose the name Attunetion and have not found another app with that name.
